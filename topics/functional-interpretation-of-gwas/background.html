Background#

Why interpretation is hard#

Genome-wide association studies (GWAS) have mapped many thousands of loci for complex traits and diseases (Visscher et al. 2017), but about 90% of the associated variants lie outside protein-coding sequence. Risk variants are strongly enriched in regulatory DNA, such as DNase I hypersensitive sites, in the cell types relevant to each trait (Maurano et al. 2012). Associations are also spread across much of the genome, as described by the omnigenic model. The main task is to go from a statistical signal to a biological mechanism: which tissues and cell types, which genes, and which pathways.

Main approaches#

  • Heritability enrichment. Stratified LD score regression (S-LDSC) partitions SNP-heritability across functional annotations. LDSC-SEG applies it to tissue- and cell-type-specific expressed genes to find the tissues that drive a trait.

  • Gene-level and gene-set analysis. MAGMA aggregates SNP associations into gene and pathway scores. Assigning SNPs to genes by position alone can miss distal regulation, but adding regulatory interactions to the mapping is not a simple fix: in a controlled analysis it only occasionally strengthened gene-set results, and it needs controls for several pitfalls and confounders (Groenewoud et al. 2022).

  • Colocalization and molecular QTLs. coloc and eCAVIAR test whether a GWAS signal and an eQTL share a causal variant. Many GWAS hits still lack a matching eQTL, partly because eQTL and GWAS studies are biased toward different kinds of genes (Mostafavi et al. 2023).

  • TWAS and PWAS. FUSION and S-PrediXcan impute expression from genotypes and test it for association; PWAS does the same for predicted protein function. Their opportunities and pitfalls, such as co-regulated genes and LD, are reviewed by Wainberg et al. 2019.

  • Locus-to-gene models. L2G combines fine-mapping, distance, QTL and chromatin features into one causal-gene score.

Deep-learning-based interpretation#

Sequence-based models (see Non-coding variant effect prediction) score how each variant changes chromatin or expression in many cellular contexts. Aggregating these scores over the variants of a GWAS links the trait to specific cellular contexts, for example in IMPACT-DL.

Open questions#

  • How to bring in cell states and developmental stages that bulk eQTL atlases do not cover.

  • How to combine many weak, correlated lines of evidence into calibrated gene and cell-type calls.
