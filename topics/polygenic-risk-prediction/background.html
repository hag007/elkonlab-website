Background#

What a polygenic score is#

A polygenic risk score (PRS) sums an individual’s risk alleles across the genome, weighted by effect sizes estimated in a GWAS (Torkamani et al. 2018; Choi et al. 2020). For several common diseases, people at the top of the PRS distribution have a risk similar to that of carriers of monogenic mutations (Khera et al. 2018).

Methods#

  • Pruning and thresholding (P+T). Keep independent SNPs below a p-value threshold, as in PLINK, PLINK2 and PRSice-2.

  • Bayesian shrinkage. Model all SNPs jointly with an LD reference: LDpred2, PRS-CS and SBayesRC.

  • Functional priors. Weight SNPs by annotations: LDpred-funct, PolyPred and SBayesRC.

  • Deep learning. Model non-additive effects or use biological structure, for example PRS-Net.

Portability across ancestries#

Most GWAS participants are of European ancestry, and PRS accuracy drops sharply in other populations (Martin et al. 2019). The drop depends on the target population: European-based breast-cancer PRSs still identify Ashkenazi Jewish women in Israel at markedly elevated risk, with an odds ratio of about 1.6 per standard deviation (Levi et al. 2023). Multi-ancestry methods combine GWAS from several populations: PRS-CSx, CT-SLEB and TL-PRS. PRANA takes a different route: a deep-learning model that adapts an existing European PRS to another ancestry using a modestly sized cohort from the target population, without a large GWAS there.

Imputation and the target population#

GWAS and PRS studies usually genotype people on SNP arrays and impute the untyped SNPs from a reference panel of sequenced individuals. Imputing a target population with a panel of its own ancestry usually improves imputation accuracy. However, when the GWAS and the target population differ in ancestry, an ancestry-matched imputation panel for the target set improves PRS prediction only marginally, and only under specific conditions (Levi et al. 2024).

Open questions#

  • Fair performance across ancestries.

  • Clinical utility when PRS is added to existing risk models (see Integrated risk prediction).
