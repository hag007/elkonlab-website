Background#

Enhancers#

Enhancers are short DNA elements, bound by transcription factors, that activate genes from a distance, often tens to hundreds of kilobases away (Shlyueva et al. 2014). Most GWAS variants fall in enhancers, so knowing which enhancer controls which gene is central to interpreting genetic risk (Elkon & Agami 2017).

Mapping enhancers#

  • Chromatin profiling marks candidate enhancers by open chromatin and histone modifications: ChIP-seq, ATAC-seq and CUT&Tag.

  • Enhancer RNAs. Active enhancers are transcribed, and these eRNAs are needed for p53-dependent enhancer activity (Melo et al. 2013).

  • Functional assays test enhancer activity directly: MPRA, STARR-seq, CRISPR-Cas9 enhancer screens (Korkmaz et al. 2016) and CRISPRi screens, up to genome-wide scale (Gasperini et al. 2019).

3D genome#

Chromosome conformation capture shows how DNA folds in the nucleus. Hi-C revealed topologically associating domains (TADs; Dixon et al. 2012) and chromatin loops anchored by CTCF (Rao et al. 2014). Micro-C, HiChIP and promoter capture Hi-C increase resolution or focus on promoters. Disrupting TAD boundaries can rewire enhancer–promoter contacts and cause disease (Lupiáñez et al. 2015). A meta-analysis of Hi-C and expression data shows how 3D organization relates to gene expression programs, at baseline and under stress (Nurick et al. 2018).

Linking enhancers to genes#

Computational models predict enhancer–gene links. FOCS and CT-FOCS use correlated activity across samples. The ABC model multiplies enhancer activity by contact frequency, and EpiMap links enhancers across hundreds of tissues.

Open questions#

  • Many genes have several redundant enhancers, which buffers them against mutations in one of them.

  • Predicting enhancer–gene links in cell types without 3D data.
